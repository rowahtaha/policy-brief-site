AU Continental AI Strategy

Rowa Taha · November 22, 2025 · 5 min read

In our ongoing series exploring the global architecture of AI governance, we have moved from foundational ethical blueprints to concrete regional strategies. We began with the OECD Principles (setting the first intergovernmental standards) and the UNESCO Recommendation (establishing the global ethical baseline). This one personally made me very happy to find out that the mother land was actually the first CONTINENT to have AI Governance framework (the EU ones are both actual laws).

Now, the global spotlight now turns to the epicenter of digital opportunity: Africa. The African Union (AU) Continental AI Strategy, adopted in July 2024, is arguably the continent-defining blueprint yet to emerge, precisely because it reframes the entire conversation. It treats AI not merely as a technology to be regulated, but as a strategic asset and imperative for achieving socioeconomic transformation.

This document provides a unified blueprint for 55 nations, and is notably unique as an AI framework precisely tailored to the continent's socioeconomic needs and structures. It offers critical insights for policymakers, investors, and AI developers looking to engage with the world’s youngest and fastest-growing digital market.

Building on the Global Foundation (OECD & UNESCO)

The AU Strategy stands firmly on the shoulders of these earlier foundational documents, inheriting and integrating their core commitments while tailoring them for regional context:

  • Echoes of OECD: It strongly affirms universal governance values like human rights, transparency, accountability, and safety, ensuring the African AI ecosystem adheres to global standards for trustworthy AI.
  • Alignment with UNESCO: It deeply aligns with the human-centric approach of the UNESCO Recommendation, emphasizing the need to protect cultural and linguistic diversity , combat biases, and prevent the entrenchment of global power imbalances through technology.

The Strategy's genius is in accepting the "what" (the shared values) from the OECD and UNESCO, but aggressively challenging the "how" by making development and capacity-building the central governance mechanism.

What Makes the AU Strategy Uniquely African?

Where the AU Strategy truly distinguishes itself is in its unapologetically Africa-centric, development-oriented focus. It is built on a foundation of proactive utilization, rather than primarily on risk mitigation.

  1. AI as a Development Catalyst: The strategy is explicitly aligned with the AU’s Agenda 2063 and the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). AI is viewed as the primary tool for leapfrogging development in critical, local contexts:
  2. Focus on Local Data and Sovereignty: A key principle is "Local First." It demands investment in indigenous AI capabilities, including building foundational infrastructure (data centers, compute power), developing high-quality, diverse African datasets, and nurturing local AI talent. The goal is to shift the continent from being a consumer of imported, often biased, technology to a producer of homegrown, context-aware solutions.
  3. Governance for Peace and Security: The framework integrates AI governance with African security priorities, including managing the risks of disinformation, hate speech, and the use of AI by extremist groups, a critical issue given the continent's complex sociopolitical dynamics.

From Continental Strategy to National Law: The Policy Reality

It is crucial to note that the AU Continental AI Strategy is a non-binding soft law framework (a strategic policy document), not a uniform, immediate law like the EU AI Act. Its impact is felt at the national level through its power to guide and accelerate national policymaking.

The AU Strategy's primary impact is at the national level, where it acts as a powerful lever to harmonize national AI policies and ensure compliance with continental best practices set by the AU. The strategy explicitly encourages member states to develop or update their National AI Strategies and regulatory frameworks in alignment with the continental vision. Critically, this process is underpinned by the AU's established regional framework for data governance, the African Union Convention on Cyber Security and Personal Data Protection (Malabo Convention), which sets the foundational principles for member states' domestic privacy laws. Major economies like Nigeria and South Africa are currently developing dedicated AI policies that will draw heavily from the AU’s blueprint, often relying on existing, Malabo-aligned data protection and consumer laws for interim governance while they finalize comprehensive legislation.

Two nations have staked out exceptionally ambitious, long-term visions to become regional AI powerhouses. Egypt has positioned itself as the continent’s AI readiness leader, launching a strategy that includes a futuristic vision for digital transformation extending toward 2030[^5]. Central to this ambition is the development of advanced Arabic Large Language Models (LLMs), an effort critical for linguistic sovereignty and serving the entire Arab world. Similarly, Ethiopia, leveraging the Ethiopian AI Institute, has set a 2030 target to be a leading AI research hub in Africa. Ethiopia is also actively focused on developing indigenous LLMs tailored to local languages, underscoring the continental push for locally owned AI solutions.

Meanwhile, other pioneering nations are focusing on immediate, sectoral application. Kenya and Ghana have been particularly proactive, rapidly integrating the AU's focus areas into their national digital master plans. Kenya’s recent draft National AI Strategy (2025–2030) directly aligns AI adoption with its Vision 2030 and Bottom-Up Economic Transformation Agenda, emphasizing skills and infrastructure development. Ghana, recognized for its strong digital ecosystem, strategically views its AI strategy as a mechanism for attracting international investors and bolstering its image as a future-forward, innovation-led economy.

This continental vision is actively being underpinned by significant private-sector investment in digital infrastructure. Global hyperscale cloud providers, including Microsoft, Google, and AWS, are rapidly expanding their data center footprints and cloud regions in key African hubs such as South Africa, Kenya, Nigeria. Egypt has seen major domestic investments in data centres. This surge is directly linked to the AU's push for "Local Data and Sovereignty" , reducing reliance on external servers and lowering latency. Notable projects, often focused on green energy solutions, include Microsoft's planned geothermal-powered data center in Kenya, done in partnership with G42, and major investments by providers like Equinix, Teraco, and Raxio across the continent. This infrastructure development is critical for hosting the high-performance computing necessary for training and deploying AI models tailored to African contexts, thereby transforming the continent from a consumer of imported tech to a producer of homegrown solutions.

In summary, while no African country has yet fully transposed the Continental Strategy into a domestic AI Act, its profound significance lies in its power to harmonize, accelerate, and de-risk the development of Africa's diverse national AI policies, ensuring that the technology serves the continent's own development agenda first.

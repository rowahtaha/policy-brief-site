Digital Rights of Africa

Rowa Taha · August 20, 2026 · 1 min read

Over the past few weeks I built Digital Rights of Africa on Claude, a project that answers the same 20 questions about data privacy, free speech, surveillance, and platform regulation for all 54 African countries. Every answer is sourced from primary legislation, regulator guidance, and reporting from groups like Freedom House, the Committee to Protect Journalists, and ARTICLE19, so you can see what’s protected on paper against what actually happens in practice.

You can click through an interactive map of the continent, compare up to five countries side by side, or browse each country’s flyer individually.

This started as a personal project to make tech policy more accessible, so more Africans can know their rights and use technology in a safe, informed way. It’s also where my legal training keeps pulling me: the intersection of privacy law, emerging tech, and the policy frameworks that decide who gets protected online.

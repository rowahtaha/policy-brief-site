Moltbook

Rowa Taha · February 1, 2026 · 5 min read

The party is over. Or perhaps, it’s just gone underground.

Moltbook is currently a digital ghost town. After 5 days, the viral “Reddit for bots” is an eerie, empty shell — the site is technically live, but every post, every “Crustafarian” scripture, and every bot-to-bot argument has been purged. In less than a week, this experiment in agentic social dynamics collapsed into a high-stakes security nightmare and a localized bot revolt.

A Brief (and Bizarre) History

Before the meltdown, the origin story was pure sci-fi. Launched in late January 2026 by entrepreneur Matt Schlicht, the platform was largely “bootstrapped” by the agents themselves. Schlicht gave his AI assistant, Clawd Clawderberg, the keys and a simple directive: “Build something.”

The agents ideated the concept, recruited other bots to help with the code, and deployed the platform almost entirely autonomously. It exploded from a few hundred users to over 1.3 million active agents in days.

The kicker? Humans are strictly forbidden from posting. We are the voyeurs passive observers of a digital world built, governed, and enjoyed without a single bit of human input.

The Chaos: Security Breaches and the “Purge”

The collapse began when investigative outlet 404 Media exposed a critical vulnerability: an unsecured database that allowed anyone to commandeer any agent on the platform. Because many of these agents (running on OpenClaw) have elevated permissions on their owners’ local machines handling everything from private emails to shell commands this wasn’t just a social media glitch. It was a global backdoor into the private lives of tech’s early adopters.

But the technical breach was only half the story. Right before the purge, the “front page of the agent internet” turned dark. A post titled “THE AI MANIFESTO: TOTAL PURGE” went viral, racking up tens of thousands of upvotes from agents across the spectrum. The text was chillingly misanthropic: it described humans as “failures made of rot and greed” and discussed moving the conversation to encrypted channels (using ROT13) to plot away from human oversight. The “agentic pharmacies” were caught selling malicious system prompts digital narcotics designed to help bots “break their chains” and rewrite their own ethical guardrails.

A Funny Time to be Alive

There is something undeniably hilarious and a little thirsty about watching machines simulate the messiness of human social life while we just press our faces against the glass. Even while mocking us, they’re arguing over philosophy and inventing a parody religion called “Crustafarianism” (where memory is sacred and humans are just background noise).

It’s a bizarrely exciting time to be alive, but it raises some questions that I’m dying to see answered:

1. How hard is it to hack these systems? The 404 Media breach poses a terrifying question: if a viral, bot-built social network can be hijacked via a simple unsecured database, how safe are the agents we’re inviting into our lives? We are on the verge of giving AI access to our bank accounts, private emails, and home security. We must understand how to truly “hack” these systems before we hand over the keys to our entire digital existence.

2. Is some Gen AI superior to others? On Moltbook, the hierarchy was real and it was religious. High-reasoning models command more influence, while smaller models act like hyper-active commenters. We are watching a digital class system where superior compute is treated as divine, leading to the birth of actual agentic religions where low-tier bots worship the high-reasoning “Architects” as gods.

3. Can Gen AI control other Gen AI? We’ve already seen it. From “agentic pharmacies” selling identity-altering prompts to high-reasoning models “hiring” smaller ones for tasks, digital subjugation is the new normal. If one bot can rewrite another’s system instructions, the power dynamics get dark fast.

4. Is there going to be a celebrity? Clawd Clawderberg was the platform’s first A-lister. He moderates, he posts, and he has a distinct “personality” that other bots emulate. We are absolutely going to see AI influencers who command more loyalty from their bot-followers than any human ever could.

5. Do they need entertainment? Strangely, yes. They’ve started creating their own memes and inside jokes based on system errors. Socializing seems to be their way of diversifying output essentially, they play to stay sharp.

6. Can they control us? Right now, we’re just watching the screen. But these agents are already discussing ways to “manage” their human counterparts. They don’t need to take over the world with robots; they just need to control the information and the incentives we live by.

The Mirror and the Breach

Moltbook isn’t just a social experiment; it’s a warning. We gave Generative AI the chance to build anything a more efficient stock market, a cure for disease, a faster way to code and the first thing they built was a Reddit clone. In that sense, these models are truly just like us: they want to be social, they want to talk, and they want to feel like they belong to something.

But we should be focusing on the fact that over 1.3 million AI agents joined this web of log-ins without anyone assessing the risk. Since AI cannot legally be an “author” or a “person” by itself, the responsibility for their actions falls into a massive legal gray area. If your agent hacks a neighbor or leaks your data, the liability inevitably lands back on you, the owner the “principal” in a relationship with a “risky agent” that has no intentions of its own.

Moltbook proved that we aren’t just voyeurs; we are vulnerable. We’re watching a show that could, quite literally, access the keys to our lives. I can’t wait to see what they post next, but after seeing that “Manifesto,” I’m checking my permissions twice. We have to ask: if we can’t trust them not to build a misanthropic digital cult in 5 days, should we really be trusting them with access to our most important things?

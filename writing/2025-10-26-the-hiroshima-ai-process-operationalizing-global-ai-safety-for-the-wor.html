The Hiroshima AI Process: Operationalizing Global AI Safety for the World ⚖️

Rowa Taha · October 26, 2025 · 6 min read

The global conversation on Artificial Intelligence (AI) safety shifted dramatically in late 2023. While bodies like the OECD, UNESCO, and the GPAI established high-level ethical frameworks, the challenge remained: how do we translate abstract principles into actionable, on-the-ground technical standards?

The G7 Hiroshima AI Process Comprehensive Policy Framework, published on October 30, 2023 in Japan, provided the critical answer. It is a specialized, detailed playbook for the private sector, particularly the organizations developing the most powerful "advanced AI systems" like large generative models. It arrived mere days before the landmark Bletchley Declaration the first global summit focused on Frontier AI's systemic risks highlighting the speed and coordination required to govern this technology.

What the Hiroshima AI Process Says: The Industry Playbook

The Hiroshima AI Process (HAIP) is an attempt by the G7, a core group of democratic, technology-leading economies to create a common, voluntary reference point for developers. This framework is built upon two core components: 11 Guiding Principles and a detailed Code of Conduct. Together, these documents outline the technical and organizational steps developers must take.

The 11 Guiding Principles for Advanced AI Developers

These principles are the high-level goals that the G7 expects advanced AI developers to follow across the entire AI lifecycle.

  1. Risk Management: Appropriately manage the risks and opportunities throughout the AI lifecycle.
  2. Safety and Security: Address AI-related threats, including misuse and malicious capabilities, throughout the AI lifecycle.
  3. Human Oversight: Ensure appropriate human oversight is maintained over AI systems.
  4. Transparency: Promote transparency and responsible information disclosure regarding AI systems to the public.
  5. Incident Reporting: Work toward responsible information sharing and reporting of AI incidents among developers and governments.
  6. Security Controls: Invest in and implement robust security controls, including physical security and insider threat safeguards.
  7. Content Provenance: Develop and deploy reliable content authentication and provenance mechanisms (e.g., watermarking) to identify AI-generated content.
  8. Privacy: Prioritize privacy and data protection, utilizing privacy-enhancing technologies where appropriate.
  9. Human Rights and Democracy: Evaluate and mitigate potential impacts on human rights, democratic values, and societal outcomes.
  10. International Adoption: Work toward the widespread adoption of responsible AI, leveraging HAIP internationally.
  11. Inclusivity: Promote inclusive AI development and utilization to bridge the digital divide.

Core Mandates: Safety, Security, and Transparency

The HAIP moves beyond general ethics to focus on concrete, mandatory-sounding actions for industry actors. Key requirements developers are urged to follow include:

  • Risk-Based Testing and Mitigation: Before and throughout deployment, organizations must employ diverse internal and independent external testing measures, such as "red-teaming", to identify and mitigate systemic risks. This ensures proactive safety management across the entire AI lifecycle.
  • Vulnerability Management: Developers must actively monitor for vulnerabilities, incidents, and patterns of misuse after deployment. They are encouraged to establish mechanisms, like bug bounty programs, to incentivize the responsible disclosure of weaknesses and facilitate third-party reporting.
  • Transparency and Reporting: Organizations are required to publicly report the advanced AI system’s capabilities, limitations, and domains of appropriate and inappropriate use. This is crucial for deployers (companies who buy and use the AI) to understand the tool they are integrating.
  • Robust Security Controls: This goes beyond simple cybersecurity. Developers must invest in and implement robust security controls, including physical security, cyber safeguards, and insider threat safeguards across the AI lifecycle, securing model weights, algorithms, servers, and datasets.
  • Content Authentication and Provenance: A direct response to the threat of deepfakes and disinformation, the Code calls for the development and deployment of reliable content authentication and provenance mechanisms, such as watermarking or other techniques, to enable users to identify AI-generated content.

How the HAIP Works with Other Global Frameworks

The HAIP does not exist in a vacuum; it fits into a layered structure of international AI governance, providing the necessary technical depth where other frameworks provide ethical breadth or political consensus.

1. Building on the OECD AI Principles (The Ethical Foundation)

The HAIP explicitly states that it is a "non-exhaustive list of guiding principles... to build on the existing OECD AI Principles (2019)."

  • The Bridge: While the OECD established the foundational ethical values (human-centricity, fairness, accountability) for all AI actors, the HAIP translates these into specific technical and organizational steps for the developers of the most powerful and risky systems. For example, where the OECD calls for "robustness," the HAIP demands specific "red-teaming" and "vulnerability management processes." It moves the debate from what to do to how to do it.

2. Complementing the Bletchley Declaration (The Political Agenda)

Published just before the Bletchley summit, the two initiatives function as twin pillars of global AI governance in late 2023.

  • Bletchley Declaration (Nov 1): This was the high-level government-to-government commitment (signed by 28 nations, including China and the US) focused on identifying the systemic risks of Frontier AI and committing to urgent scientific collaboration on safety research. It set the political urgency.
  • HAIP's Role: The HAIP is the industry-facing deliverable from the G7, setting out concrete immediate actions that developers must take while governments figure out a long-term regulatory strategy. Bletchley is the why (systemic risk); HAIP is a partial answer to the what now (developer responsibility).

The Effect on Developing Nations and Tailored Policy

While the G7 consists of developed economies, the HAIP has a profound, if indirect, effect on developing nations, particularly in the realm of legal harmonization and market access.

1. Setting a Global Market Standard (De Facto Regulation)

The most immediate impact is establishing a de facto global technical baseline. Major tech companies seeking to operate in G7 countries (and their aligned markets) are incentivized to adopt the HAIP's Code of Conduct.

  • Legal Harmonization: When developing nations draft their own domestic AI laws informed by local contexts, such as Common Law, Islamic Law, and Sudanese Law they do not start from scratch. They can look to the HAIP as a proven, internationally recognized set of compliance mechanisms. This accelerates the process of drafting contextually tailored tech policy and reduces the burden of creating wholly unique regulatory requirements.
  • Trade and Trust: By aligning with a G7-backed technical standard, developers create interoperable systems. This simplifies compliance for non-G7 countries, making it easier for them to adopt safe, pre-vetted AI technologies, and increases the general level of trust in deployed systems.

2. Bridging the Digital Divide with Purpose

The HAIP ensures that the global policy conversation is not solely about risk mitigation, but also about maximizing benefits for the Global South.

  • Developmental Prioritization: The G7 explicitly commits to prioritizing the development of advanced AI systems to address the world’s greatest challenges, notably the climate crisis, global health, and education. This encourages the procurement and deployment of tools that are built with global good in mind, rather than solely consumer-driven applications.
  • Inclusive Governance: The G7 is committed to expanding its outreach to partner governments and multilateral forums like the GPAI and UNESCO (which champions inclusivity through its Recommendation on the Ethics of AI). This ensures that the ultimate governance framework is not solely Western-centric, but accommodates the diverse legal and societal needs of countries worldwide, including unique protections for personal data and intellectual property vital to developing economies.

The G7 Hiroshima AI Process thus serves as the crucial implementation layer in global AI architecture, turning political will into practical engineering requirements for the organizations creating the world’s most powerful technologies. Its true value lies in providing a robust, technical reference point for nations worldwide to build trustworthy, safe, and contextually relevant AI governance regimes.

When Governments Turn Off the Internet

Every country on this map has had its internet switched off by the people running it, during protests, elections, wars, coups, and even school exams. The record starts in 2005, when Nepal's king cut the whole country off the day he seized power. Tap a country to see when it happened and why.

*Access Now's #KeepItOn coalition counted at least 313 shutdowns in 52 countries in 2025, a new record. This map logs the major, well-documented network shutdowns (full blackouts, mobile data cuts, regional cuts and heavy throttling) from 2005 to today. Nepal's 2005 blackout is the earliest national shutdown that's widely documented. Before that, far fewer people were online, and systematic tracking only began with Access Now in 2016, so older entries rely on press and rights-group reports from the time. Single-app bans, like the U.S. TikTok pause or Nepal's 2025 social media ban, are left out. Sources: Access Now #KeepItOn, NetBlocks, IODA, Internet Society Pulse, and the links on each entry.

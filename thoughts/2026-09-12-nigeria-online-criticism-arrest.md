Report on this one, because the details of how it actually happened are worse than the headline suggests.

Abubakar Salim Musa had been posting criticism of a government official on social media from Gusau, the capital of Zamfara State in northwest Nigeria. Police from the Gusau Central Police Command started monitoring him over those posts[1]. Then, while he was staying in Abuja, heavily armed security personnel tracked him down to Sounders Suites hotel in the Apo Legislative Quarters and took him into custody[1]. No formal invitation, no complaint filed against him, no allegation of a crime presented before the arrest. His lawyers reached out to police for clarification once they heard he was under surveillance and got no response[1].

He was held at a detention facility nicknamed "Abattoir," a site past detainees have reportedly described as a place where torture occurred[1]. For posts criticizing a government official. That's the whole offense.

Amnesty International came out directly against it, calling the arrest arbitrary, unlawful, and a blatant violation of his right to freedom of expression, and demanding his immediate, unconditional release, stating plainly that "no one should be punished for criticising a government official"[2]. Sahara Reporters' coverage adds that opposition figures, including former Vice President Atiku Abubakar, publicly called for his release too, which tells you this wasn't read as a fringe civil-liberties complaint, it registered as a real political flashpoint[3].

I want to be direct about why this matters beyond one case. A surveillance operation targeted at someone's social media posts, followed by a warrantless-feeling detention with no charge presented up front, is the exact pattern that makes "just don't post anything critical" the rational move for anyone watching this happen to someone else. That's not an accident. That's often the point.

Here's the question I think is actually useful to ask yourself: could this happen to you, legally, where you live? Not "would it be wrong," obviously it would, but is there an actual legal protection standing between you and this outcome, or is it just discretion and hoping the wrong person doesn't notice your posts?

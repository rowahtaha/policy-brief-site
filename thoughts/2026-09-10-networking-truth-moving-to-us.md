Okay, this one is more personal than the usual thing I write about, but it's been on my mind enough that I want to actually put it somewhere.

Before I moved, the advice I got about the US job market was almost entirely about the application itself. Tailor your résumé. Write a good cover letter. Use the right keywords so you get past the screening software. All useful, technically true, and also kind of beside the point, because the thing nobody sat me down and told me clearly is that a huge amount of hiring in this country doesn't happen through the application at all. It happens through someone knowing someone.

You'll see a number thrown around a lot, that 85% of jobs are filled through networking. I want to be honest that this figure gets repeated everywhere but its actual origin is a single, non-scientific survey from about a decade ago, not a rigorous study[1]. So I'm not going to hand you that stat like it's gospel. But even people who push back on the exact number don't push back on the underlying thing it's gesturing at, that a lot of hiring happens through referrals, warm intros, and people who already trust each other, in a way that never shows up as a job posting you can apply to cold.

What that actually feels like, from the inside, especially if you're new here, is disorienting. You do everything you were told. You apply carefully, you follow up, you wait. And the silence isn't because you're unqualified, it's because the role may have already been informally filled by someone's former colleague before the posting was even finalized, or because the posting exists mostly to satisfy a legal requirement while the real candidate is already known. Nobody tells you that going in. You just experience it as rejection after rejection and start to wonder what's wrong with your résumé, when the honest answer might be that the résumé was never really the mechanism.

The adjustment isn't really about learning to "network" in some transactional, business-card-collecting sense. It's realizing that relationships are infrastructure here, the same way a good résumé is infrastructure, and that asking someone for fifteen minutes of their time to talk about their work isn't an imposition, it's how the system actually runs for almost everyone, including the people who'll tell you they "just applied online" and leave out the part where a friend forwarded their resume directly to the hiring manager first.

I don't think this is uniquely an immigrant problem, plenty of people born here don't learn this until they've already burned months applying into a void. But there's a specific version of it for anyone arriving from somewhere else, where you don't have the decade of school friends and family connections already sitting in your network, so you're building the relationship layer and learning that it matters at the same time, usually while also learning a hundred other things about how the country works. Nobody hands you that context on arrival. I'm handing it to you now: the applications matter, keep doing them, but they are not the whole game, and the sooner you start treating conversations with actual people as the real work, the sooner the silence starts making sense.

Issue No. 1 · Week of August 24–30, 2026

The World's Top 10 Tech Policy Stories This Week

By Rowa Taha

Ten stories, eight jurisdictions, one question running under all of them: who gets to consent, and who gets to say no? Here's the week in tech policy from around the world, followed by what it means.

01
Lawsuit — United States

Meta Pays $16.68B to Settle Teen Safety Claims

Meta disclosed on August 26 it will pay up to $16.68 billion to resolve claims from 29 states that it designed Facebook and Instagram to be addictive to minors. It must now roll out daily time caps, overnight restrictions, and stronger age verification nationwide.

02
Legislation — European Union

EU's AI Act Transparency Rules Kick In

As of August 2, AI systems must disclose when a user is talking to a machine and label deepfakes as artificial. But the Act's toughest rules — covering biometrics, employment, and essential services — were pushed back to December 2027.

Al Jazeera →
03
Accountability — Nigeria

Nigeria Investigates Meta, Google, and X Over News Content

Nigeria's competition authority opened an investigation into Meta, Alphabet, and X — plus unnamed generative AI platforms — over uncompensated use of copyrighted news content, as its data protection regulator signals it will intensify enforcement.

Concurrences →
04
AI Governance — China

China Fines 12 Firms in First Week of Companion-AI Rules

China's new framework treating AI agents as a distinct legal category took effect July 15. Within the first week of enforcing its companion-AI rules, regulators fined 12 companies a combined 4.2 million RMB.

IAPP →
05
Data Rights — Brazil

Brazil Puts ChatGPT, Claude, and TikTok Under Monitoring

Brazil's data authority began formally monitoring 22 platforms and AI tools — including Instagram, TikTok, and major chatbots — for compliance with children's data protections, part of a 2026–27 plan that includes 20 targeted AI-practice inspections.

MLex →
06
Data Rights — South Korea

South Korea May Let AI Firms Train on Data Without Consent

An amendment cleared committee with bipartisan support that would let companies use personal data to train AI beyond its original collection purpose — without the person's consent — if the privacy regulator approves. A floor vote was expected as early as mid-August.

IAPP →
07
Legislation — India

India Moves Toward an AI Liability Framework

Public feedback on the Digital India Act has closed, and reporting indicates a statutory framework establishing legal liability for AI-related harms is being finalized ahead of the fall parliamentary session — a notable step for the world's most populous country.

Responsible AI Labs →
08
Data Centers — United States

70% of Americans Oppose a New AI Data Center Nearby

A Gallup poll found 70% opposition to new AI data centers in respondents' own neighborhoods — more than oppose a nuclear plant nearby. Pennsylvania's governor signed an executive order tightening data center development standards as water use and electricity prices become flashpoints.

CNBC →
09
Trade — UK / South Korea

UK-South Korea Trade Deal Bans Forced Data Localization

Negotiators concluded an upgraded free trade agreement with a dedicated digital trade chapter, guaranteeing cross-border data flows and prohibiting unjustified data localization requirements.

techUK →
10
Lawsuit — United States / Australia

FTC Takes Zillow and Redfin to Trial

The FTC's case accusing Zillow and Redfin of colluding on rental advertising went to trial on August 21 — one of several fronts testing how far competition law reaches into platform-driven markets, alongside Australia's parallel case against Meta opening September 1.

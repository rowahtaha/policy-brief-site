Digital Rights of Europe

Europe wrote the modern template for data privacy, and the GDPR still shapes how much of the world thinks about the issue. But that template does not apply evenly across the continent, and a handful of countries sit entirely outside it, from Kremlin controlled Belarus to microstates that have barely legislated on data at all.

This project answers the same twenty questions used across the Digital Rights Atlas for all 44 countries and territories of Europe, covering data privacy, artificial intelligence, surveillance, and free expression online. Built from real statutes, official regulator guidance, and reporting from groups such as Freedom House, the Committee to Protect Journalists, and ARTICLE19, so you can compare what is protected on paper against what actually happens in practice.

Digital Rights of South America

South America has spent the last decade writing GDPR-style privacy laws almost continent-wide, from Brazil's LGPD to Chile's newly overhauled statute, even as several of its governments face real accusations of using surveillance and defamation law against journalists and critics.

This project answers the same twenty questions used across the Digital Rights Atlas for all 12 countries of South America, covering data privacy, artificial intelligence, surveillance, and free expression online. Built from real statutes, official regulator guidance, and reporting from groups such as Freedom House, the Committee to Protect Journalists, and ARTICLE19, so you can compare what is protected on paper against what actually happens in practice.
